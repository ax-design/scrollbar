Scrollbar

Scrollbars you style with CSS, on a scroll view and on the page.

Windows 8 is the default look: square parts, a 17px bar, flat ticks and a square card. These scroll views set no look at all: only their content, snap (whole device pixels) and no touch input (the touch indicator is a panning indicator).

Reading

Slow mornings

Most days start the same way. The kettle goes on before the window is open, and the house is quiet for a while. It is a good time to read the notes from the day before and to decide what needs doing first.

Some people like a long list. Others prefer three things written on a card. Either works when the list is kept short enough to look at once.

Keeping a shared table

A table that many people use needs a few simple habits. Things go back where they were found. Anything that is finished goes in the box by the door, and anything that is not yet finished gets a label with a date on it.

Nobody should have to ask where the scissors are. A drawer for small tools, kept in the same place, saves more time than any clever arrangement of shelves.

Walks without a plan

A walk with no destination often ends up somewhere better than planned. Take a turn that looks interesting and see where the street leads. Stop when the light starts to change, not when the phone says the walk is finished.

Bring water and something to sit on. A bench is a fine place to end a walk, and the trip home is shorter when there has been a rest.

Plants on a windowsill

Most indoor plants want bright light and very little water. Too much of the second kills more plants than anything else. Push a finger into the soil before watering, and water only when the top layer is dry.

Turn the pot a quarter turn each week so that the plant grows evenly. Leaves that turn yellow are usually a sign of too much water, not too little.

Cooking for one

Cooking for a single person means buying small amounts or freezing what is left. Soup, rice dishes and roasted vegetables all keep well for several days, and they are easy to reheat without much attention.

Write down the meals that worked. A short list of five or six dinners is enough to avoid the same disappointment twice in a week.

Letters and short notes

A short handwritten note can say more than a long message. It does not need to be clever. Saying that a thing was noticed, or that a day was missed, is usually enough to start a reply.

Keep a few blank cards near the desk. A card that is written in five minutes is more likely to be sent than one that waits for the right moment.

Tools that stay sharp

A dull knife is more dangerous than a sharp one, because it asks for more force and slips more easily. Sharpen blades a little and often, rather than rarely and heavily.

Oil wooden handles once or twice a year. Store tools dry, and check them after a wet season for rust on the joints and the edges.

Quiet evenings

The last hour before sleep is easier with the lights low and the screens off. A book, a radio or a conversation with someone in the same room will do.

When the evening ends, put tomorrow's first task on top of the pile. That makes the morning start a little more gently than the one before.

Settings

Windows 11 keeps its scrollbars out of the way. At rest a scrolling list shows only a thin line, and the track and the arrows come when the pointer is over the bar.

Accessibility

Under Accessibility there is a switch named Always show scrollbars. With it on, every list shows the full bar, and it stays: the Full variant of this page.

The track is acrylic: it blurs what scrolls under it. The swatches above and below are there to show that, as they pass under the bar.

Markers

The headings of this text are markers on the bar, small ticks in the accent colour. Move the pointer to the bar and they come forward; hover one for its card, press it to go there.

A tick is wider the nearer it is to the pointer, white on the thumb, and the one for the section on screen is the strongest.

Timing

The bar expands 400ms after the pointer arrives, in 167ms, and contracts 500ms after it leaves. Those times, and the ones for the fades, are tokens of the scroll view, read from the WinUI source.

Black market

Prices change when you look at them. A vendor with three arms and one working eye will tell you it is the last unit in the city, and he has said so since the winter.

Notes on this page

Each stage is a scroll view, or a window with one, and its bars are drawn by the component. The stages show three ways to make a look. Windows 8 is the default look: nothing to write. Cyber Punk, Minimal and Windows 11 style the default parts with ::part() rules (cyberpunk.css, minimal.css, win11.css) and tokens on the scroll view; Windows 11 also adds a layer by configuration: its track names the acrylic layer of @ax-design/acrylic among its layers (track-layers on the element). Windows XP is dependency injection: a kit (@ax-design/scrollbar/kits/luna) that replaces the parts themselves, with classes that carry their own look.

The page scrollbar is a separate element. It is placed over the page, and the native scrollbar of the page is hidden while it is shown. The select in the header picks its look: Windows 8 (the default), Minimal, the Windows XP kit, Windows 11 in each of its variants or Cyber Punk.

Markers

Headings in the content become markers on the track. Hover one to see its card, or click it to scroll to that section. The stage on the left uses the second-level headings, and the window on the right uses the third-level ones.

Markers in the Windows 8 stage are flat ticks. In the Windows XP stage they are etched lines, as on the slider marks of that look.

Using the examples

Try the wheel, the keyboard and a touch screen if you have one. Drag the thumbs, click the tracks and hold the buttons. Each of these actions should behave like the native bar would.
